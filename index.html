About

I build the control arm that decides whether a result is real.

Ricky Gole is a machine learning researcher completing an M.S. in Advanced Computing at Morgan State University (4.0 GPA, graduating December 2026). His research centers on the empirical evaluation of machine learning systems: finding the failure modes that stay hidden in benchmark scores and only surface once a model is deployed. That work spans LLM evaluation, educational retrieval, clinical NLP, reinforcement learning, and cloud security.

In 2026 he had three first-author papers accepted at IEEE ICDM, EDM, and IEEE Cloud Summit, plus a co-authored ACM Learning@Scale paper, following a co-authored IEEE BIBM 2025 paper on safety-constrained reinforcement learning. His findings include clinical LLM ensembles that converge on the same wrong answer 19.9 times more often than chance, drug-interaction models that collapse from 0.73 to 0.00 F1 on post-2019 FDA data, a directional scoring bias against African American English in automated essay graders, and knowledge-tracing benchmarks where a two-feature baseline recovers a third of a deep model's gains. Each diagnosis is paired with a fix, such as a safety routing rule with 96.1% precision, a hybrid BM25 + FAISS retrieval probe, or a signal-gated fusion architecture that stays robust to noisy modalities.

His approach is to build the control arm that decides whether a result is real. He runs multi-arm ablations designed to falsify his own hypotheses, ships reproducibility releases that recompute every headline number from raw predictions, and builds multi-agent systems that verify their own citations and decline to act when the evidence does not support it. Before graduate school, he earned a B.S. in Computer Information Systems and Financial Economics from Caldwell University and worked in SOC monitoring and infrastructure operations, experience that still shapes his focus on systems that hold up under real-world conditions.

Convergent Failure Auditing of Clinical LLM Ensembles

  • Evaluated five models (Llama-3-8B, Qwen-2.5-7B, Gemma-3n, GPT-4o, DeepSeek-V3) on 4,089 MedQA and MedMCQA questions. Models pick the same wrong answer far more than independence predicts (24.6% unanimous-wrong vs. 1.23% chance, a 19.9x gap).

Provenance-Conditioned Clinical Encoder (Falsification Study)

  • Falsified a provenance-conditioning hypothesis in a from-scratch clinical transformer encoder: across seven architecture arms and three seeds, an arbitrary segment-index bias (3.4078 val_loss) beat true authorship labels (3.5310).
